# Change how text looks
<!-- keywords: mini toolbar, floating toolbar, character spacing, expanded, condensed, raised, lowered, position, kerning, set as default, default font, font, font box, font dialog, typeface, size, style, bold, italic, color, underline, underline color, strikethrough, double strikethrough, superscript, subscript, small caps, all caps, hidden, hidden text, effects, preview, ctrl+d, character formatting, character shading, shading, character border, border, box around letters, colour, underline colour -->

The **Home > Font** group has buttons for the common changes: font, size, bold, italic, and so on. The **Font** box has all of them in one place, and a few more.

## The mini toolbar

When you select words with the mouse, a small bar floats above them. It has the buttons you use most: **Bold**, **Italic**, **Underline**, make the text smaller or bigger, left, centre, and right alignment, bullets, and numbers. They are the same buttons as on the ribbon, so you can change the look without moving the mouse far. The bar goes away when you click elsewhere or type.

## The font list

Click the arrow next to the font name on the ribbon to open the list. It shows the fonts of the document's look first, then the five fonts you picked last, then every font on your computer. Each name is drawn in its own font, so you can see what it looks like before you choose it. Press **Ctrl+]** to make the text a little bigger and **Ctrl+[** to make it a little smaller.

## Open the Font box

Select the text to change. Drag across it, or double-click a word. Then do one of these:

- Press **Ctrl+D**.
- Click the small button with three dots in the corner of the **Font** group.
- Right-click the text and click **Font…**.

If nothing is selected, your choices apply to the text you type next.

## What the box changes

- **Font** is the typeface, such as Times or Arial. The first two choices are the document's heading font and body font. They follow the document if you change its fonts later.
- **Font style** is **Regular**, **Italic**, **Bold**, or **Bold Italic**.
- **Size** is the height of the letters, in points. There are 72 points in an inch.
- **Font colour** is the colour of the letters. **Automatic** leaves the colour to the document.
- **Underline style** has a single line, a double line, a thick line, dotted and dashed lines, and a wavy line. **Words only** leaves the spaces between words without a line.
- **Underline colour** is the colour of the line. **Automatic** uses the colour of the letters.

The **Preview** at the bottom shows how the letters will look.

## Effects

- **Strikethrough** draws a line through the letters. **Double strikethrough** draws two.
- **Superscript** raises small letters above the line, as in the 2 of m². **Subscript** lowers them, as in the 2 of H₂O.
- **Small caps** shows lowercase letters as small capital letters. **All caps** shows every letter as a capital. The text you typed does not change, so you can turn these off again.
- **Hidden** keeps the text in the document but does not show it or print it. To show hidden text again, select the text around it (press **Ctrl+A** to select everything), open the **Font** box, and click **Hidden** until its box is clear.
  To only look at hidden text for a while, turn on **Show/Hide ¶** (Ctrl+Shift+8). The hidden words then show with a dotted line under them. Turn it off, and they hide again. Nothing in the file changes.

Some effects cannot go together: both kinds of strikethrough, superscript with subscript, and small caps with all caps. Choosing one clears the other.

## Character spacing

The **Character spacing** part of the box changes the room between letters.

- **Spacing** can be **Expanded** (letters farther apart) or **Condensed** (closer together). **By** says how many points.
- **Position** can be **Raised** or **Lowered**. This moves the letters up or down from the line, by that many points, without making them smaller.
- **Kerning** pulls pairs of letters such as A and V closer so that the gap looks even. Check the box and give the smallest size, in points, at which to do it.

## Make the choices the default

Click **Set as Default** and then **Yes**. The font, size, style, and colour you chose become those of the document's Normal style, so they are used for all text that has no other choice. The text you selected takes them too.

## When the selected text is not all alike

Select text that has more than one font, style, size, or colour. The box for that is then blank, because there is no one answer. If you leave it blank, each letter keeps what it had. Choose a value in it to give that value to all of the selected text.

## A box that is half filled

Select text that is partly bold, or partly struck through, or has some other mix. The box for that effect then shows a filled square. It means that some of the text has the effect and some does not.

JeffWrite changes only what you change. If you leave a box alone, each letter keeps what it had. Click a box until it shows a check mark to give the effect to all the text, or until it is clear to take it away from all of it.

## Undo and saving

Click **OK** to change the text. All the changes are one step, so one **Undo** (Ctrl+Z) takes them all back. Click **Cancel** to leave the text as it was.

JeffWrite saves every effect in the .docx file, and other programs show them the same way.

## Shading and a border behind letters

The **Home > Font** group has two buttons for marking letters without changing them.

- **Character Shading** (the paint bucket) colours the space behind the selected letters. Pick a colour, or **No Shading** to take it away. It is not the same as a highlight. A highlight is the marker pen, and shading is a fill that belongs to the letters. If you use both, the highlight shows.
- **Character Border** (the box) draws a thin line round the selected letters. Click it again to take the line away.

Both go in the file, so the letters look the same when someone else opens it.
