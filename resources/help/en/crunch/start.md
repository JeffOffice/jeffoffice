# Welcome to JeffCrunch
<!-- keywords: welcome, getting started, introduction, beginner, first steps, quick start, overview, what is JeffCrunch, basics, preview, spreadsheet, workbook, sheet, cells, what works, not yet, limits -->

JeffCrunch is a program for spreadsheets. It is free and open source. There is no fee, no subscription, and no account to set up. It is one of the JeffOffice apps, and it runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.

The file you work on is called a **workbook**. A workbook holds one or more **sheets**, and each sheet is a grid of **cells**. JeffCrunch opens .xlsx and .xlsm workbooks, including those made by other programs.

## JeffCrunch is an early Preview

This version shows workbooks, and it does not change them yet. You can open a workbook, look at every sheet, select cells, copy them as text, zoom, and freeze rows and columns for your own view. Typing in cells, formatting, sorting, charts, saving, and printing come in later versions. The ribbon shows those commands now, dimmed, so you can see where they will go.

## Do these three things first

1. **Open a workbook.** Press **Ctrl+O**, or click **File** and then **Open**. The first rows appear at once, even in a very large workbook, while JeffCrunch reads the rest.
2. **Look around.** Click a cell to select it. Its address is in the **Name box** at the top left, and what it holds is in the **formula bar** next to it. Use the arrow keys, the mouse wheel, and the scroll bars to move. See [Select cells and move around](select-and-move).
3. **Change the view.** Use the **View** tab to zoom, to turn the gridlines and headings on or off, and to freeze the top rows or the first columns. See [Change the view](view).

## What you see

A workbook looks as it was saved: the widths of the columns and the heights of the rows, the fonts, colors, fills, and borders, merged cells, frozen panes, and the number formats that turn a stored number into a date, a percent, or a price. Text that is too long for its cell spills into the empty cells beside it. A number that is too wide for its cell shows as a row of # signs; select the cell to read the number in the formula bar (changing the width of a column comes in a later version).

## Keep a copy

Because this is a Preview, keep a copy of any workbook that matters. JeffCrunch never writes over a file, but it does not save yet.
