# Welcome to JeffCrunch
<!-- keywords: welcome, getting started, introduction, beginner, first steps, quick start, overview, what is JeffCrunch, basics, preview, spreadsheet, workbook, sheet, cells, what works, not yet, limits -->

JeffCrunch is a program for spreadsheets. It is free and open source. There is no fee, no subscription, and no account to set up. It is one of the JeffOffice apps, and it runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux.

The file you work on is called a **workbook**. A workbook holds one or more **sheets**, and each sheet is a grid of **cells**. JeffCrunch opens .xlsx and .xlsm workbooks, including those made by other programs.

## JeffCrunch is an early Preview

This version opens workbooks, lets you type in cells, and saves them. You can open a workbook, look at every sheet, select cells, copy them as text, zoom, and freeze rows and columns for your own view. You can type numbers, text, and formulas in cells, clear cells, undo and redo, and save the workbook. You can also format cells (fonts, colors, borders, number formats), cut and paste, insert and delete rows, columns, and sheets, and change the size of columns and rows. You can set up how a sheet is cut into pages, see the pages before they print, print them or save them as a PDF (see [Print and make a PDF](printing)). Sorting and more come in later versions. The ribbon shows those commands now, dimmed, so you can see where they will go.

## Do these three things first

1. **Open a workbook.** Press **{key:Open}**, or click **File** and then **Open**. The first rows appear at once, even in a very large workbook, while JeffCrunch reads the rest.
2. **Look around.** Click a cell to select it. Its address is in the **Name box** at the top left, and what it holds is in the **formula bar** next to it. Use the arrow keys, the mouse wheel, and the scroll bars to move. See [Select cells and move around](select-and-move).
3. **Change a cell.** Select a cell and type. Press **Enter** to keep what you typed. See [Type in cells](edit-cells).
4. **Format cells.** Select cells and use the **Home** tab to change the font, colors, borders, and number format. See [Format cells](format-cells).
5. **Change the view.** Use the **View** tab to zoom, to turn the gridlines and headings on or off, and to freeze the top rows or the first columns. See [Change the view](view).

## What you see

A workbook looks as it was saved: the widths of the columns and the heights of the rows, the fonts, colors, fills, and borders, merged cells, frozen panes, and the number formats that turn a stored number into a date, a percent, or a price. Text that is too long for its cell spills into the empty cells beside it. A number that is too wide for its cell shows as a row of # signs; widen the column, or select the cell to read the number in the formula bar. Numbers are written the way your computer's language writes them (see [Number formats and your language](number-formats)), and a workbook's conditional formats, such as color scales, data bars, and icons, are drawn (see [Conditional formats](conditional-formats)).

## Keep a copy

Because this is a Preview, keep a copy of any workbook that matters. **Save** writes your changes into the workbook you opened, and **Save As** writes them into a new file. See [Open and save workbooks](open-save).
