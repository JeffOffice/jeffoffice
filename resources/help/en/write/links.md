# Add links and text from other files

## Links

A **link** (also called a hyperlink) is words that take you somewhere when you Ctrl+click them. It can go to a web address, to an email address, or to a **bookmark** in the same document.

1. Select the words that will be the link. Or leave nothing selected and type the words in the box **Text to display**.
2. Press **Ctrl+K**, or choose **Insert > Link**.
3. Choose **Web or email address** and type the address. You can leave off "https://": JeffWrite adds it. An email address gets "mailto:" added. Or choose **Place in this document** and pick a bookmark. (To make a bookmark, use **Insert > Bookmark**.)
4. Type a **Screen tip** if you like. It is the small note that shows when the pointer rests on the link.
5. Click **OK**. If the address is not one JeffWrite can use, the window stays open and says why.

The words get the link look (blue and underlined). It is one step to undo.

To **change** a link, put the caret in it and press Ctrl+K again. **Remove Link** in that window, or **Insert > Remove Link**, takes the link off and keeps the words.

**Ctrl+click** a link to follow it. A link to a bookmark moves the caret there. A link to a web or email address opens your browser or mail program. For your safety, JeffWrite follows only web, email, FTP, and telephone links. It never starts a program or opens a file because of a link.

## Text from a file

**Insert > Text from File** puts the text of another file in your document, where the caret is. It can be a document (.docx, .doc, .rtf, or .odt) or a plain text file. A document keeps its fonts, styles, pictures, and links. In a plain text file, each line becomes a paragraph. A file can be up to 64 megabytes. It is one step to undo.
