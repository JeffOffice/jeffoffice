# Make a table of authorities
<!-- keywords: table of authorities, authorities, citation, mark citation, case, statute, rule, regulation, treatise, passim, legal brief, TA, TOA, Alt+Shift+I, category -->

A **table of authorities** is a list that a legal brief has: the cases, laws, and other works the brief cites, with the pages that cite them. You first **mark** each citation, and then you **insert** the table.

## Mark a citation

1. Select the citation in your text, such as the name of a case with its numbers.
2. Click **References > Mark Citation**, or press **Alt+Shift+I**.
3. Check the **Long citation**. This is what the table shows. Type a **Short citation** if you use a shorter form later in the text.
4. Pick a **Category**: Cases, Statutes, Other Authorities, Rules, Treatises, Regulations, or Constitutional Provisions. There are nine more numbered categories for your own use.
5. Click **OK**.

Mark each place that cites the authority. The table lists the authority once, with all the pages.

## Insert the table

1. Click where the table should go.
2. Click **References > Table of Authorities**.
3. Pick a category, or **All** to list every category that has marks. Choose the tab leader, whether to use **passim**, and whether to put the category name above its citations.
4. Click **OK**.

**Passim** is a Latin word for "here and there." When an authority is cited on five pages or more, the table shows the word instead of a long list of numbers.

## Keep the table up to date

Click in the table and click **References > Update List** (in the Table of Authorities group), or press **F9**.
