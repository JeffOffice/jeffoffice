# Make an index
<!-- keywords: index, mark entry, mark all, subentry, cross-reference, see, XE, INDEX, page range, bold page number, columns, run-in, letter headings, Alt+Shift+X, update index -->

An **index** is an alphabetical list of words and ideas with the pages they are on. You first **mark** the words that belong in it, and then you **insert** the index.

## Mark an entry

1. Select the word or words to list, or click where the entry should point.
2. Click **References > Mark Entry**, or press **Alt+Shift+X**.
3. Check the **Main entry**. Type a **Subentry** if the entry has a part under it. For example, the main entry `Apples` with the subentry `Fuji`.
4. Choose what the page number shows:
   - **Current page** lists the page the mark is on.
   - **Page range** lists the pages that a bookmark covers, such as 5-7.
   - **Cross-reference** lists words instead of a page, such as "See Fruit."
5. Turn on **Bold page number** or **Italic page number** if the number should stand out.
6. Click **Mark** to mark this place, or **Mark All** to mark every place where the same words (with the same capital letters) appear.

A mark is a hidden **index entry field** (an XE field). It shows nothing on the page.

## Insert the index

1. Click where the index should go.
2. Click **References > Insert Index**.
3. Choose the look: **Indented** puts each subentry on its own line, and **Run-in** puts the subentries after the entry, on one line. Choose whether to **Right align page numbers**, the tab leader, and whether a letter heads each group.
4. Click **OK**.

The index lists entries in alphabetical order. Page numbers of one entry are listed once each, in page order, separated by commas.

## Keep the index up to date

Click in the index and click **References > Update Index**, or press **F9**. Marks that you added are listed, marks you deleted are gone, and the pages are worked out again.
