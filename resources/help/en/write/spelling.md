# Check your spelling
<!-- keywords: spelling, spell check, spell checker, misspelled, red line, wavy line, squiggle, suggestions, ignore, ignore all, add to dictionary, custom dictionary, dictionary, language, proofing, f7, review, check spelling as you type, uppercase, numbers, grammar, grammar check, style, blue line, dotted line, check grammar as you type -->

JeffWrite checks your spelling as you type. A word that is not in the dictionary gets a red wavy line under it. The check runs on your own computer, with no internet connection, and it knows 31 languages and regional forms.

## While you type

- A red wavy line means the dictionary does not have the word. The word may still be right. Names and new words are often missing. The names of the JeffOffice apps (JeffWrite, JeffPub, JeffDeck, JeffOffice, JeffCrunch, JeffMail, and JeffBase) and LLC are never marked, in any language, with or without an 's.
- The word you are typing is left alone until you move on, so a half-typed word does not flicker.
- The lines show on the screen only. They never print, and they never go into a PDF.

To fix a word, **right-click** it. The menu shows these choices above the usual ones:

- Up to six **suggestions**, the best one first, in bold. Click one to put it in place of the word. It is one step, so **Ctrl+Z** takes it back.
- **Ignore All** leaves that word alone, wherever it appears, until you close JeffWrite.
- **Add to Dictionary** teaches JeffWrite the word for good. The word goes in a small file in JeffWrite's own folder, so you can read or edit your list of added words there.

If the dictionary has no suggestion, the menu says so.

## Step through the misspellings

Press **F7**, or click **Review > Spelling**. The Spelling pane opens on the right. It shows the first misspelled word after the caret, selected in your text, with the line it is in and a list of suggestions.

- **Change** puts the chosen suggestion in place of the word. Double-click a suggestion to do the same.
- **Change All** does that for every copy of the word in the document. It is one step, so **Ctrl+Z** takes back all of them.
- **Ignore** passes this word by. Its red line stays.
- **Ignore All** and **Add to Dictionary** work as they do in the right-click menu.

The pane goes to the end of the document, then continues from the top, up to the place where it began. When no more are left, it says that the spelling check is complete. The pane goes on to grammar and style issues when the spelling is done (see Check grammar, below).

## Turn the red lines off or on

Click **Review > Check Spelling as You Type**. The same choice is in **File > Options > Proofing**, and it is the one choice all the JeffOffice apps use. There you can also choose to skip words in UPPERCASE and words with numbers in them, such as 3rd. Both are skipped at first.

## Check grammar as you type

JeffWrite also looks for clear grammar and style mistakes in English text. An issue gets a blue dotted line under it. Spelling keeps its red wavy line, and a misspelled word is never marked as a grammar issue. These are the mistakes it looks for:

- a repeated word ("the the")
- "a" and "an" before the wrong sound ("an test", "a hour")
- a sentence that starts with a small letter after a period
- "its" and "it's", "their" and "there", "your" and "you're", "then" and "than", and "lose" and "loose", where the words around them make the mistake clear
- "could of" for "could have"
- two spaces in a row inside a sentence
- a space before a comma, a period, or another mark that belongs to the word before it
- no space after the end of a sentence
- "alot", and "everyday" for "every day" ("almost everyday")
- verbs that do not agree with the subject in clear cases ("she don't", "they was", "it have", "everyone are", "there is many reasons")
- the wrong form of a verb ("did went", "has went", "can't hardly", "don't have no")
- words that are mixed up ("who's car", "an affect on", "less people", "irregardless", "can't breath", "weather" for "whether", "chose" for "choose", "advise" for "advice")
- "between you and I", "Me and him went", "more better", and "these thing"
- a comma after the year of a date ("On June 5, 2020, we left"), a comma after a city and state ("in Austin, Texas, the heat"), and a hyphen in a compound before a noun ("a well-known author")
- a small "i" for the word "I", and the days and months in small letters

It would rather miss a mistake than mark text that is right, so a sentence with no blue line is not a promise that it is correct.

Right-click the issue. The menu shows what is wrong, then one or more suggestions. Click a suggestion to put it in the text, and press Ctrl+Z to take it back. **Ignore This Issue** removes the line from this one place, until you close JeffWrite. The same words somewhere else keep their line, and editing elsewhere in the paragraph does not bring it back. **Ignore All** removes the line from every issue with this message on these words.

The check runs on your own computer, with no internet connection, in the background: typing is never held up, and a paragraph is checked a moment after you stop typing in it. A paragraph is checked as far as its first 100,000 characters. A document that takes more checking than the limit stops being checked, and the status bar tells you once; spelling goes on working.

Only English is checked. In a paragraph with more than one language, the English text is checked and the rest is skipped, and so is text that a file marks "do not check". Grammar is checked in the text of the document, and not yet in tables, headers, footers, or notes.

To turn it off, click **Review > Check Grammar as You Type**. The same choice is in **File > Options > Proofing**. It is one choice for all the JeffOffice apps, and it is the same in every window. The Spelling pane (F7) goes through the grammar issues after the spelling, when this is on.

**Choose what it looks for.** Click **File > Options > Proofing > Grammar settings** to pick the kinds of issues: **Grammar** (verbs that do not agree, the wrong form of a verb, "a" and "an"), **Punctuation** (extra or missing spaces, commas, and hyphens), **Capitalization**, **Mixed-up words** (words that sound alike), **Repetition**, and **Style**. **Style** is off until you turn it on. It suggests, and it is not about mistakes: sentences of more than 40 words, the passive voice, three sentences in a row that begin with the same word, and wordy phrases ("in order to", "due to the fact that"). The choice is kept when you click **OK** in Options. It applies at once to the blue lines and to the Spelling pane (F7) in the open windows of this app, and to the other JeffOffice apps the next time they start.

## Languages

JeffWrite checks each piece of text in its own language. A file can say what language its text is in, down to a single word, and JeffWrite reads that. Text that says nothing is checked in the document's own language. The file gives that language for the whole document, and a new document uses American English.

Text in a language that JeffWrite has no dictionary for is not checked. Neither is text that a file marks "do not check spelling". You can't change the language of text in JeffWrite yet, but it keeps what the file says.

The 31 dictionaries are English (American, British, Canadian, and Australian), Spanish (21 regions, including Mexico and Spain), French, German, Italian, Dutch, and Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal).

## What is not checked yet

Red lines show in the text and in the cells of tables. They do not show in headers, footers, notes, or text boxes yet, and the Spelling pane does not check them.
