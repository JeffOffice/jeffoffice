# Add and change pictures

A **picture** can sit in a line of text, like a letter, or float on the page with the words going round it. This topic shows how to put a picture in a document, how to change its size, its place, and how the text goes round it, and how to describe it for people who can't see it.

## Put a picture in

- Click **Insert > Pictures**, then **Picture**, pick a file, and click **Open**. JeffWrite reads PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, TIFF, WebP, and SVG files. A picture that comes from a file is kept in the document as the file it was (a WebP or SVG picture is kept as PNG).
- Or click **Insert > Pictures > Picture from Clipboard**, or press **Ctrl+V**, when you have copied a picture (a screenshot, say). The picture is kept in the document, so it is still there when you save and open the file again.
- Or drag a picture file from your computer's file manager onto a page. The picture goes where you drop it.
- Or click **Insert > Icons**. A box shows small drawings of things, such as arrows, people, and tools. Type a word to find one, pick one or more, and click **OK**. Each icon goes in at the caret as a picture about two thirds of an inch square.
- Or click **Insert > Barcode** to put in a barcode (see below).

The picture goes where the caret is, in place of anything you selected. It is made as large as it asks to be, but no wider than the column and no taller than the page's text, with its proportions kept. The picture is selected afterward, and the **Picture Format** tab appears at the right of the ribbon.

A picture that is too large (more than 40 million pixels, a side longer than 30,000 pixels, or a file of more than 48 MB) is not put in, and the status bar says so. So is a file that is not a picture.

## Find free pictures online

**Online Pictures** finds free pictures. They come from two open libraries: Openverse, which searches many collections, and Wikimedia Commons, the library of Wikipedia's pictures.

1. Click **Insert > Pictures**, then **Online Pictures…**. A pane opens on the right.
2. Choose a library, type what you are looking for, and click **Search** (or press Enter).
3. Click a picture to see who made it and what license it has. A **license** says how you may use a picture. Click the license's name to read it.
4. Click **Insert** (or double-click the picture).

**What JeffWrite sends.** Your search words go to the library you choose, and only when you click **Search** or press Enter. JeffWrite then asks the library for the small previews of what it found, and for the one picture you insert. Like any web page, the library can see your computer's internet address, and it is told that JeffWrite is asking. Nothing from your document is sent.

The picture goes in at the caret, in line with the text, and is kept in the document. Its title is saved as its **alt text**. Most licenses ask you to name the author. When **Add a credit under the picture** is checked, JeffWrite adds a small line of text under the picture with its title, its author, and its license. It is a paragraph you can change or delete. Inserting is one step, and **Undo** takes back the picture and its credit together. Check that the license fits how you will use the picture.

## Change the size

Click the picture to select it. Eight small squares, the **handles**, appear on its corners and sides.

- Drag a **corner** handle to make the picture larger or smaller in the same proportions.
- Drag a **side** handle to change one side only.
- Or type a number in **Picture Format > Height** or **Width**. With **Lock Aspect Ratio** on, the other number follows. Pictures are held between 2 points and 2,000 points on a side.

Each change is one step to undo.

## Crop a picture

To **crop** a picture is to cut off part of its edges. Select the picture and click **Picture Format > Crop**. Type how much to cut off the left, top, right, and bottom, as a percent of the whole picture. Click **OK**.

The picture frame gets smaller by the same amount, so the part that is left keeps its size. Crop again later and you start from the whole picture, so you can always get back what you cut. A number below zero leaves empty room around the picture. The cut on two opposite sides can add up to no more than 98 percent.

## Cut a picture to a shape

Select the picture and click **Picture Format > Crop to Shape**. Pick a shape, such as an oval, a star, or a rounded rectangle. The picture is shown only inside that shape. Pick the plain rectangle to put the corners back. The picture file itself is not changed.

## Turn a picture

Select the picture and click **Picture Format > Rotate**. Turn it a quarter turn to the right or the left, or back to no turn.

## Line pictures up

Click **Align** on the **Picture Format** tab. Choose **Align Left**, **Align Center**, or **Align Right** to line the picture up across the page, and **Align Top**, **Align Middle**, or **Align Bottom** to line it up down the page. By default the picture lines up with the margins. Turn on **Align to Page** to line it up with the edges of the page instead. A picture that sits in the line of text becomes a floating picture in front of the text.

## Change how a picture looks

These commands are on the **Picture Format** tab. Each one is a single step to undo, and none of them changes the picture file inside your document.

- **Corrections** makes the picture brighter or darker, and gives it more or less contrast. **Brightness** is how light the picture is. **Contrast** is how different the light parts are from the dark parts.
- **Color** turns the picture to shades of gray (**Grayscale**), to pure black and white (**Black and White**), or to a pale, faded look (**Washout**). **No Recolor** puts the colors back.
- **Picture Styles** and **Picture Border** put a frame round the picture. Pick a style, or pick a color, then a **Weight** (how thick the line is) and **Dashes** (a solid, dotted, or dashed line). Choose **No Border** to take the frame off.
- **Change Picture** puts a different picture in the same place. The new picture keeps the width of the old one, its place on the page, how the text goes round it, and its alt text. Its height follows its own shape.
- **Reset Picture** takes off the crop, the corrections, the color change, and the border. The picture comes back to the scale it has now.

## Make pictures smaller

A picture from a camera can be much larger than it shows on a page, and it makes the file large. Click a picture, then click **Compress Pictures…** in the **Adjust** group of the **Picture Format** tab. Choose where the document will be used: **Commercial printing (300 ppi)**, **Desktop printing (220 ppi)**, or **Web (96 ppi)**. Leave **Apply to all pictures in the body of the document** checked to make every picture smaller, or clear it to make only the selected picture smaller. **Delete cropped areas of pictures** also takes away the hidden parts you cropped, and the crop is gone. A picture is only replaced when the new one is smaller. Its size, place, wrap, and alt text stay as they were. Undo (Ctrl+Z) gives the pictures back, and the file holds only the smaller ones when you save.

Only pictures in the body of the document are made smaller. Pictures in a header, a footer, a text box, or a shape stay as they are, and so does a picture that is a drawing (SVG, EMF, or WMF) or an animation.

## Wrap text round a picture

**Picture Format > Wrap Text** says how the words go round the picture. You can also right-click the picture and choose **Wrap Text**.

- **In Line with Text**: the picture is a character of its line, and moves with the words around it.
- **Square**: the words go round a rectangle that holds the picture.
- **Tight**: the words go as close to the picture as a rectangle allows (JeffWrite treats it as Square).
- **Top and Bottom**: the words stop above the picture and start again under it.
- **Behind Text**: the picture is under the words, which go straight over it.
- **In Front of Text**: the picture is over the words, which go straight under it.

## Put one picture over another

Pictures that float (any wrap except **In Line with Text**) lie in layers, like sheets of paper. **Bring Forward** moves the selected picture up one layer. **Send Backward** moves it down one layer. Each button has a small menu with **Bring to Front** and **Send to Back**, which move it over or under all the others. The same four commands are in the right-click menu, under **Arrange**.

A picture at the very bottom of the layers in front of the text goes behind the text on one more **Send Backward**. A picture behind the text comes out in front of it on one more **Bring Forward**. A command is dimmed when the picture cannot move that way. Each one is a step that **Undo** takes back.

## Move a picture

Drag the picture. A picture **in line with text** goes to the place in the words where you let go. A picture with any other wrap moves to where you let go on the page, and stays there while the words around it change (it stays with the paragraph it belongs to, on that paragraph's page).

Dragging a picture that is in line with text does not change its wrap; to let it float, choose a wrap such as **Square**.

## Describe a picture

A picture that only decorates the page, such as a line or a border, does not need a description. Select it and click **Picture Format > Mark as Decorative** (or right-click it). Screen readers then skip it. Click it again to take the mark off.

**Alt text** is a short description that screen readers read aloud, and that shows when a picture can't be. Select the picture and click **Picture Format > Alt Text** (or right-click the picture and choose **Alt Text**), type the description, and click **OK**.

## Pictures from other documents

JeffWrite shows and keeps the pictures of the documents it opens. It can change the size, wrap, place, and alt text of a picture that the document stores as a plain picture. A picture stored in a form JeffWrite doesn't change (a group of drawings, for instance) is selected and kept as it was; its commands are off.

## Put in a barcode

A **barcode** is a pattern of bars that a scanner reads. JeffWrite makes book ISBNs (with or without a price), EAN-13, UPC-A, EAN-8, Code 128, Code 39, and QR codes. A QR code holds any text, such as a web address.

1. Put the caret where the barcode should go, and click **Insert > Barcode**.
2. Choose a **Type** and type the text or digits. The window shows the barcode as you type, and says in red what is wrong if it can't be made.
3. Click **Insert**.

The barcode goes in as a floating picture, with the text wrapping around it. Drag it to move it, drag a corner to resize it, and use the **Picture Format** tab to change how the text wraps. Keep **White background** on, because a scanner needs the clear space around the bars. To change the barcode, double-click it (or select it and click **Insert > Barcode** again), change the text, and click **Update**. It keeps its place and its width. Each change is one step you can undo.

When you save, the barcode is saved as a picture, with an SVG that stays sharp, so every program shows it. JeffWrite also keeps the barcode's settings in the file, so when you open the file in JeffWrite again, it is a barcode you can still edit. In other programs it is an ordinary picture. Its alt text says what it holds.
