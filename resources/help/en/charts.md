# Charts

A **chart** turns numbers into a picture, such as columns, a line, or slices of a pie. This topic shows how to put a chart on a page, how to type its numbers, and how to change how it looks.

## Put a chart on a page

1. Go to the page where the chart should go.
2. Click **Insert > Chart**, and pick a kind: column, stacked column, 100% stacked column, bar, stacked bar, line, line with markers, pie, doughnut, area, stacked area, or scatter.

A **stacked** chart piles the series on top of each other. A **100% stacked** chart makes each stack the same height, so you see shares. A **scatter** chart puts each point where its two numbers say.

The chart comes in with sample numbers, in the middle of the page, and the **Chart Format** tab opens. A chart is an object like a picture or a shape: drag it to move it, drag a handle to change its size (the chart is drawn again to fit, so its words stay the right shape), and turn it with **Rotate** on the **Arrange** group.

## Type your own numbers

Select the chart and click **Chart Format > Edit Data**. A table opens.

- The first column holds the words under the points (the **categories**), such as the months.
- The first row holds the name of each **series**, which is one set of numbers, such as one product.
- The other cells hold the numbers.

Click **Add Row** or **Add Column** for more, or **Remove Last Row** or **Remove Last Column** for fewer. Click **OK**. If a cell is not a number, the chart is not changed, and the status bar tells you which row. A chart can have up to 200 categories and 24 series.

For a scatter chart, the first column holds the numbers that place the points from left to right.

## Change how it looks

These commands are on the **Chart Format** tab. Each one is a single step to undo, and the chart keeps its colors and the rest of its look.

- **Change Chart Type** turns the chart into another kind with the same numbers.
- **Chart Title** shows or hides the title. **Edit Chart Title** changes its words.
- **Legend** is the key that says which color is which series. Put it at the right, top, left, or bottom, or take it off.
- **Axes** shows or hides the line of words under the chart (the **categories**) and the line of numbers beside it (the **values**), and sets a title for each. A pie chart has no axes.
- **Gridlines** draws faint lines across the chart to help your eye follow the numbers. The **major** lines sit at the main numbers on the side, and the **minor** lines sit between them.
- **Chart Styles** picks one of eight overall styles.
- **Data Labels** writes each number on its column, point, or slice.

## Add trendlines, error bars, and colors

Select the chart. Three small buttons stand beside it, and the same commands are on the **Chart Format** tab.

- **Chart Parts** (the plus button) adds or takes away a part of the chart: the title, labels, a table of the numbers, and more. Its **Trendline** entry draws a line through the points that shows where they are heading (linear, exponential, and other kinds), and can write the line's equation on the chart. Its **Error Bars** entry draws a small bar at each point for how far the true number may be from the one shown: a fixed amount, a percentage, or a spread of the numbers.
- **Layouts** picks one of several ready-made placements of the title, legend, and labels.
- **Colors** changes the colors the series are drawn in. **Looks** (the brush button) picks an overall look.
- **Chart Filters** (the funnel button) hides a series without deleting its numbers.
- **Format Chart** opens a window for the look of one part: the whole chart, the plot area, or one series. **Format Horizontal Axis** and **Format Vertical Axis** set the range, steps, and number format of an axis.
- **Chart Data** and **Swap Rows and Columns** change which numbers are the series and which are the categories.

Each of these is one step to undo.

## Saving, printing, and PDF

- A **.jpub** file keeps the chart as a chart, so you can open it later and change its numbers.
- A **.pub** file can't hold a chart that other programs open as a chart. JeffPub saves the chart as a picture of it, and tells you how many charts it saved that way. Keep a **.jpub** copy if you want to change the numbers later.
- **PDF** and printing draw the chart as lines, shapes, and real text, not as a picture, so it stays sharp at any size.
