# Shapes, pictures, tables, and Text Art
<!-- keywords: shapes, lines, arrows, connectors, curves, freeform, scribble, pictures, images, insert picture, online pictures, openverse, wikimedia commons, credit, license, alt text, icons, svg, table, grid, rows, columns, text art, chart, smartart, diagram, equation, movie, video, sound, audio, play, playback, poster, speaker, control bar, trim, loop, volume, mute, full screen, group, arrange, front, back, rotate, flip, fill, outline, select, move, resize, drag and drop, picture colors, brightness, contrast, gray, black and white, duotone, recolor, transparent color -->

Besides text, a slide can hold shapes, pictures, and tables. This topic shows how to add them and what JeffDeck does with other kinds of objects.

## Draw a shape or a line

1. Click **Home > Drawing > Shapes** (or **Insert > Illustrations > Shapes**). A gallery opens. At the top are lines, arrows, elbow and curved connectors, curves, and freehand drawing. Below are shapes, sorted by kind.
2. Click one, then drag on the slide to draw it.
3. To type in a shape, select it and press Enter, or start typing.

Select a shape or a line and use **Shape Fill** and **Shape Outline** on the **Home** tab to color the inside and the edge. Each opens the colors of the presentation's theme, and has **No Fill** or **No Outline**.

## Add a picture

Click **Insert > Images > Pictures**, pick one or more files, and click **Open**. JeffDeck puts each picture in the middle of the slide. It is no larger than most of the slide. The picture's data is saved inside the presentation, so the file is complete when you share it.

You can also drag picture files from your file manager onto a slide. A picture lands where you drop it.

JeffDeck reads PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, TIFF, WebP, SVG, WMF, EMF, ICO, and PDF pictures.

## Add a picture from the web

**Online Pictures** finds free pictures. They come from two open libraries: Openverse, which searches many collections, and Wikimedia Commons, the library of Wikipedia's pictures.

1. Click the arrow under **Insert > Images > Pictures** and choose **Online Pictures…**. A pane opens on the right.
2. Choose a library, type what you are looking for, and click **Search** (or press Enter).
3. Click a picture to see who made it and what license it has. A **license** says how you may use a picture. Click the license's name to read it.
4. Click **Insert** (or double-click the picture).

**What JeffDeck sends.** Your search words go to the library you choose, and only when you click **Search** or press Enter. JeffDeck then asks the library for the small previews of what it found, and for the one picture you insert. Like any web page, the library can see your computer's internet address, and it is told that JeffDeck is asking. Nothing from your presentation is sent.

The picture goes in the middle of the slide. It is no larger than most of the slide. The picture's title is saved as its **alt text**, which is words that a screen reader says aloud for people who can't see it. Most licenses ask you to name the author. When **Add a credit under the picture** is checked, JeffDeck adds a small line of text under the picture with its title, its author, and its license. It is a text box you can move or change. Check that the license fits how you will use the picture.

## Add an icon

An **icon** is a simple picture, like a heart, an arrow, or an envelope. JeffDeck has more than 1,500.

1. Click **Insert > Illustrations > Icons**.
2. Type a word in the search box, like **arrow** or **mail**. The icons that match stay.
3. Click an icon. To pick several, hold **Ctrl** or **Shift** while you click.
4. Click **Insert** (or double-click an icon).

Each icon goes on the slide as a picture one inch square, in the middle. Several icons go side by side. They are drawn in the theme's text color. Icons are SVG pictures. That means they stay sharp when you make them bigger. You can move, resize, and turn them like any picture. When you save, each icon is saved as a picture and as an SVG, so other programs that draw SVG keep it sharp too.

## Add Text Art

**Text Art** is fancy words: letters with color, an outline, a shadow, or a curved shape.

1. Click **Insert > Text > Text Art**. A gallery of styles opens.
2. Click a style. The Text Art goes in the middle of the slide, and a box opens with its words all selected.
3. Type your words. They take the place of the old ones. Pick a font, a size, **Bold**, or **Italic** if you like. Click **OK**.

Text Art keeps its shadow when you save the presentation and open it again. To change the words later, double-click the Text Art, or select it and press Enter. You can move, resize, turn, and delete it like any object. The letters stretch to fill the box, so drag its handles to make it wider or taller.

## Add a table

A **table** is a grid of rows and columns. Each box in the grid is a cell, and each cell holds its own text.

1. Click **Insert > Tables > Table**. Slide across the grid to pick the number of rows and columns, then click. Or choose **Insert Table…** and type the **Number of columns** and **Number of rows**.
2. The table appears in the middle of the slide, with its first cell ready to type in. Type, and press Tab to go to the next cell. Press Shift+Tab to go back. Press Tab in the last cell to add a row.

While you type in a cell, the font, size, color, and alignment buttons on the **Home** tab work on its text. JeffDeck has no table tools yet for merging cells, borders, or table styles, but tables made elsewhere show as they were made.

## Add a movie or a sound

A **movie** or a **sound** plays right on the slide. JeffDeck keeps the file inside the presentation, so the movie or sound travels with it.

1. Click **Insert > Media > Video** and choose **Video on This Device…**. Or click **Insert > Media > Audio** and choose **Audio on This Device…**.
2. Pick a file and click **Open**. JeffDeck reads the file for a moment. Then it puts the movie or sound on the slide.

A movie shows its **poster**. That is its first picture. A sound shows a picture of a speaker. You can move, resize, turn, and delete them like any picture. Both start in the **click sequence**. That is the list of steps the slide show plays, one at each click. So the movie or sound plays at a click in the slide show.

JeffDeck plays the kinds of files the computer knows how to play, such as .mp4, .mov, .avi, .wmv, .mkv, .webm, .mp3, .wav, .m4a, .wma, .ogg, and .flac. A file the computer can't play shows a message where it should play. The rest of JeffDeck goes on working. A movie that a file only links to, on the web or on a disk, isn't played. The file has to be inside the presentation.

JeffDeck never plays a file until you ask. Opening, saving, and printing a presentation use only the poster.

### Play a movie or a sound while you edit

Click the movie or sound, or point at it. A **control bar** shows under it:

- The **play** button starts it. While it plays, the same button pauses it.
- The **seek bar** shows how far it has played, and the time. Click on it to jump to that spot.
- The **speaker** button mutes it, and the small slider next to it sets the volume.

The movie plays right on the slide, and it gets bigger and smaller when you zoom. Only one plays at a time. It stops when you go to another slide.

### Set how it plays

Select a movie or sound. The **Playback** tab shows. It sets how the movie or sound plays in the slide show: when it starts, how loud it is, where it starts and stops, and more. Press **F1** on that tab for its topic.

### Movies and sounds in the slide show

In the slide show, a movie or sound plays when the file says it should:

- **In Click Sequence**: it plays at a click, with the slide's animations. The click that plays it doesn't move the show on.
- **Automatically**: it plays by itself when its turn comes. A first one starts when the slide appears.
- **When Clicked On**: it plays when you click it.

Point at a movie to show its control bar. Click the movie to play it or pause it. The movie plays in its own place on the slide, or it fills the screen if **Play Full Screen** is on. It stops when the slide changes, unless it was set to play across slides.

## Select, move, and arrange objects

- Click an object to select it. Hold **Shift** and click to add more. Or drag a box around objects on an empty part of the slide. Press **Tab** to step from one object to the next.
- Drag an object to move it. Hold **Shift** to move it only straight across or straight up and down. Hold **Ctrl** while you drag to make a copy.
- Drop one picture on top of another, and the two pictures trade places. Hold **Ctrl** while you drag to make a copy instead.
- Press the arrow keys to nudge the selected object. Hold **Alt** for the smallest step.
- Drag a handle to resize. Pictures keep their proportions at a corner. Hold Shift to stretch them freely. For other objects, hold Shift to keep the proportions.
- Drag the round handle above an object to turn it.
- Press **Delete** to remove the selected object.
- Right-click an object, or press **Shift+F10**, for a menu with Cut, Copy, Paste, Delete, Edit Text, Group, Order, a link, and a new comment.

**Home > Drawing > Arrange** has **Bring to Front**, **Bring Forward**, **Send Backward**, and **Send to Back** to change which object is on top. It also has **Group** (Ctrl+Shift+G) and **Ungroup**, which make several objects move and resize as one, and **Rotate Right 90°**, **Rotate Left 90°**, **Flip Horizontal**, and **Flip Vertical**.

Turn on **View > Show > Ruler**, **Gridlines**, or **Guides** for help placing objects. **File > Options > View** has **Snap objects to other objects**. See [View tab](tab-view) and [Change JeffDeck's settings](options).

## Objects JeffDeck shows but can't make yet

A presentation made elsewhere can hold things JeffDeck has no tools for yet. It shows them as well as it can, and it keeps them when you save.

- **Text Art** from an older file is shown and saved, and you can change its words, as with Text Art you add. JeffDeck has no tools yet to change its style or shape once it is on the slide. To try another style, add new Text Art.
- **Charts** are drawn from the values saved in the file, and saved whole. You can't edit them yet.
- **Changes to a picture's colors** are shown and kept. Another program can make a picture brighter or darker (**brightness**), or make the light and dark parts differ more or less (**contrast**). It can make a picture gray, or only black and white, or make one color clear. A **duotone** gives a picture two colors: one for its darkest parts and one for its lightest. JeffDeck draws the picture with these changes, the way the other program does. When you save, the changes go back into the file as they were. The picture itself is never changed, so nothing is lost. An OpenDocument file (.odp) can keep only a few of these changes, such as brightness and contrast. If you save as .odp, JeffDeck makes the others part of the picture. You can't make these changes in JeffDeck yet.
- **SmartArt diagrams** are drawn the way they were saved. A SmartArt diagram is a set of shapes that work together, like a row of boxes or a chain of steps. JeffDeck treats the whole diagram as one object. You can select it, move it, resize it, copy it, and delete it. The drawing grows and shrinks with it. You can't change its words yet. When you save, the diagram goes back into the file whole, so the program that made it can still edit it. A copy you paste is only a picture of the diagram. A duplicated slide keeps the whole diagram.
- **Equations** are shown as the picture the file keeps of them. An equation is math set in special type, like a fraction or a square root. You can move, resize, turn, copy, and delete the picture, but you can't change the math yet. When you save, the math goes back into the file as it was. This works for a text box that has other words around an equation too.
